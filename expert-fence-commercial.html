Repair, don't replace

Rusted At The Base?
You Probably Don't Need A New Fence.

Ornamental steel and aluminum fences almost never fail across the whole panel. They fail in the last six inches — where the post meets the concrete, where water sits, where salt collects. Most contractors quote a full replacement because it is easier to sell. We cut out the failure, splice in new material, refinish it, and hand you back a fence that looks new for a fraction of the cost.

Rusted Post Base Repair

We cut back to sound metal, sleeve or splice a new base section, weld it out, and re-seat it in fresh concrete with proper drainage so it does not rot again from the inside.

Picket & Rail Replacement

Individual pickets, rails and channel sections replaced and colour-matched. Nobody can tell which section was repaired once the finish is on.

Structural Welding

Fence Condition Survey

For property managers and HOAs with long runs: we walk the whole perimeter and mark what needs repair now, what can wait a season, and what genuinely needs replacing.

Repair makes sense when

  • Panels and pickets are sound but bases are rusting
  • Damage is limited to a run or a few sections
  • The finish can be matched or the whole line refinished
  • The original profile is discontinued and you want to keep the look
  • Budget or board approval will not carry a full replacement this year

Replacement makes sense when

  • More than roughly half the run has failed
  • The fence no longer meets current code and cannot be brought up
  • Footings have heaved across the whole line
  • You are changing height, style or security level anyway
  • Repeated repairs have already exceeded replacement cost

We will tell you which one you are. Our survey says plainly what should be repaired and what should be replaced — including when replacing is the honest answer. That is why property managers keep our number.
